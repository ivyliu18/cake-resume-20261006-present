One habit, four competencies

What I noticed · A user-journey comparison

What happens right after an AI diagnosis?

I lined up two journeys that both start with the same kind of moment (a user arrives with a problem, a system diagnoses it) and tracked what happens right after.

What I noticed · The gap

AI can see the problem. It can't see the person.

Cake's AI Resume Health Check does two things well every day: score a resume, flag its weaknesses. But after diagnosis, there is no next step. That's not my guess, it's Cake's own product language.

Set beside AmazingTalker, which does the same "diagnosis" job, a human shows up immediately afterward. That contrast is the gap. It's also the single thing Part 2 and Part 3 are both going to respond to.

One sentence to carry forward: the diagnosis is not the problem. The empty space right after it is.

Part 3 · Quantum Leap: Core Argument

The same gap, at company scale

The bridge already named it: diagnosis with no next step, set against AmazingTalker's human-first model. One more layer makes it urgent: recruiter contingency fees (15–25% of first-year salary, ~20% typical) structurally exclude entry-level and career-changing candidates: Cake's largest, least-monetized user segment.

The mechanism: use health-check weakness scoring to route people into a coach-matching, revenue-share marketplace, instead of leaving them to figure it out alone.
Part 3 · Why this is provable, not a guess

The six-point case

1International precedent
Uber proved a P2P match + commission model scales without owning the supply side; coaching marketplaces (BetterUp, Coaching.com) proved people already pay for matched career coaching.
2Local precedent
AmazingTalker already runs this exact mechanic in Taiwan: tutor-set pricing, declining commission, now a career-coaching category.
3How Cake pushes it
Three-phase roadmap: 2-week concierge test → semi-automated + international tiers → full marketplace. Detail next.
4How it doesn't become the main line
Stays an add-on to the resume platform, not a rebrand. Owned by Growth Ops validation, no new department. Public story stays "job platform."
5New partner channel
Mandarin-language schools & education providers can take the same shape as Cake's Campus Ambassador programme: a coach-supply channel and an inbound channel to overseas Chinese-speaking job seekers.
6Cost & execution
NT$95–105K Phase 1 cost, zero engineering spend, two-week test, detailed on the next few slides.
Part 3 · Not Invented From Scratch

AmazingTalker already proved the mechanism

Tutor sets price, student books and pays, platform takes a declining commission (8–38%), scaled from language tutoring into K-12, music, and now career coaching from NT$200/session.

Cake doesn't need to invent a new model. It connects a proven one to something AmazingTalker structurally can't offer: real job listings. An AmazingTalker user still has to go apply on their own. Cake is already a job platform; coach matching plugs straight into real openings.

Part 3 · Validation Design

How success gets measured

One sentence to test: will coaches serve low-score job seekers for revenue share, and will those job seekers accept being referred?

  • Supply-side: 8–10 Taiwan coaches, ladder-pricing ask: a real number, not "sounds interesting."
  • Demand-side: 20–30 job seekers, testing opt-in rate for "want a real coach to look at it?"
  • Pass bar: ≥3 of 8–10 coaches (≈30%) quote a number or accept a trial → Phase 2. Below 1–2 → adjust incentives, not direction.
  • Time & cost: two weeks, zero engineering spend, one person's time.

Part 3 · Risk & Governance (raised proactively)

What I'd watch for, before anyone asks

Closest precedent: BloomTech (formerly Lambda School), misrepresented placement rates, sold students' future income shares to hedge funds, drew CFPB enforcement and class-action suits.

  • Explicit opt-in for referrals, never pre-checked.
  • Any external conversion claim needs real data behind it, never a story first, numbers later.
  • Placement/conversion rate tracked from day one (Launch Academy's 2024 enrolment pause is the cautionary case).
  • Stays a side-channel: an add-on to the resume platform, never the headline. If it ever competes with Cake's core identity for attention, that's a signal to slow down, not scale up.
